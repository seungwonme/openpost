title: Reply to Instagram messages
description: Answer an Instagram direct message that contains your keyword, once, while the messaging window is open.
icon: MessagesSquare

Use this workflow when people message your account with a keyword such as `GUIDE`. It sends your saved text to the person who asked. It does not need an AI provider and it never messages anyone who has not messaged you.

## Before you start

Connect an Instagram professional account and turn on **Messages** for it. The trigger reads messages stored in Inbox, so the account's messaging permissions and Meta webhook subscription must already deliver them. Without webhooks, OpenPost still checks the account's messages at least every 15 minutes while an active message workflow watches it, instead of backing off to every six hours on a quiet account.

## Create the workflow

Open **Workflows → Templates → Reply to Instagram keyword messages**. The draft opens in the workflow editor.

1. Open the trigger and choose the Instagram account.
2. Enter keywords separated by commas. Matching ignores capitalization, and the default matches whole words or phrases. Leave the field empty to match every message.
3. Open the **Reply to message** step and write the message, up to 1,000 bytes. Variables such as `{{source.author_name}}` count after they render.
4. Keep **Review in Inbox** while you check the wording, or choose **Send automatically**.
5. Select **Publish workflow** to activate it. Creating, saving and previewing the draft send nothing.

## Narrow the trigger

Open **Configure** in the trigger to exclude keywords, choose whole-word, exact or contains matching, change the daily run limit, or adjust reply priority. Lower priority numbers answer first when several reply workflows match the same message, and only one replies. Equal priorities use the oldest workflow. A workflow that sends nothing can still run independently.

Only messages created and first stored after activation start runs. Earlier messages, messages first loaded by scrolling back through a conversation's history, your own messages and messages from another account you connected do not. A new message that appears in history because its delivery is late still counts as new. Reactivating a paused workflow starts with future messages again. New messages start a run within about five minutes.

## Review or take over

Open the conversation in **Inbox → Messages** to see each automatic message and its state. Workspace administrators can edit a message waiting for review and approve it. Editors can select **Take over**.

**Take over** stops pending automatic messages and every run for that conversation, including a run in a wait step. So does a reply sent by hand, in OpenPost or in the Instagram app, whether or not a run is active. Automation stays stopped in that conversation until someone selects **Resume automation**, which applies to future messages. Runs that stopped stay stopped. Messages already being sent may still be delivered.

**Pause** stops new runs and leaves existing runs active. **Stop automatic replies** stops new runs and pending sends.

## What stops a message

Before each automatic message, OpenPost reads the conversation from Instagram and checks the result, so a recent request to stop is seen even if its webhook has not arrived. If Instagram cannot be read, OpenPost holds the message and retries for up to 10 minutes, then stops with a reason. It also checks after a review or a queue delay. The run fails and shows the reason when any of these is true:

- Instagram's messaging window closed. The window normally lasts 24 hours from the person's latest message. OpenPost does not use message tags to extend it.
- The person opted out.
- A person took over the conversation.
- The person deleted the message.
- The account lost its connection or its messaging permission.

A delay of 24 hours or more before a reply step fails when you publish, because that message could never be sent.

### Opting out

OpenPost sorts a message that mentions stopping into two groups. Capitalization, spacing and punctuation are ignored.

**A definite refusal** has nothing else in it. It opts that person out of automatic messages from the account, and it also covers that account's comment automation. It stays until someone removes it, and pending messages are cancelled. A refusal more than 30 days old, first found while reading history, creates no opt-out.

| Language | Definite refusals                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| English  | `stop`, with `please`, `now`, `it` and similar words. `stop` followed by `replying`, `reply`, `messaging`, `message`, `messages`, `msg`, `msgs`, `texting`, `texts`, `sending`, `send`, `contacting`, `contact`, `dm`, `dms`, `dming`, `spam` or `spamming`, which `me` or `us` can follow. `unsubscribe`, `unsub`, `opt out`, `remove me`, `leave me alone`, `no more messages`, `do not message me` and `do not text me`. |
| Korean   | `그만`, `그만해요`, `그만 해주세요`, `그만 보내주세요`, `보내지 마세요`, `연락하지 마세요`, `연락 주지 마세요`, `연락 마세요`, `수신거부`, `수신거부합니다`, `스탑` and `스톱`, with or without spaces, and with `제발`, `이제` or `좀`.                   |

**A possible request** is a short message, up to six English words or 16 Korean characters, with the same words and other content, a question or a negation. `수신거부 어떻게 하나요`, `이제 그만 고민할게요`, `how do i unsubscribe`, `can you stop`, `stop this`, `stop all`, `stop me`, `stop?` and `알림 끄는 법` are examples. So is a refusal with an emoji, such as `stop it 😂`, and `구독취소` or `구독 해지해주세요`, because cancelling a subscription can be about a paid plan. So are `cancel`, `cancel it`, `cancel this`, `cancel all`, `cancel please`, `거부`, `차단` and `알림 꺼주세요`, because they do not say what to stop. OpenPost records no opt-out, sends nothing further in that conversation, and hands it to a person with the reason on the conversation. Someone then reads the message and chooses **Resume automation** or leaves it stopped. This does not end by itself, and it does not affect comment automation.

`stop by the shop`, `don't stop`, `can't stop`, `bus stop`, `next stop is seoul` and words like `그만큼` and `그만두` are neither. A longer message that mentions these words is neither too, so automation keeps running and a refusal inside a long message can be missed. Ordinary business is neither too, and automation keeps running: `cancel my order`, `can i cancel`, `주문 취소 cancel 가능한가요`, `차단 해제 어떻게 해요`, `카드 거부`, and reports of a phone setting such as `알림 꺼졌어요`, `알림 꺼놨어요` and `알림꺼짐`.

A message in either group never starts a workflow. Automation does not read meaning, so a person checks every message in the second group.

To remove an opt-out, open the conversation or the person's comment in Inbox and choose **Remove opt-out**. This needs Edit permission. It applies to that person on the account for both comments and direct messages, and OpenPost records who removed it and when. It starts nothing by itself, stopped runs stay stopped, and a new refusal from the person opts them out again.

### Taking over

A person replying in Inbox or the Instagram app takes over the conversation. Automation stops there, and active runs are cancelled. This takeover ends seven days after that person's last message, and Inbox shows the date. Choosing **Take over** or **Resume automation** yourself is a decision that does not expire: a takeover you pressed lasts until someone resumes it.

A message the account sent more than 24 hours ago does not start a takeover. Instagram can report OpenPost's own send under another ID. OpenPost treats a message with the same text within an hour of a send as that send's echo, once per send, so a person who repeats the exact text of an automated message within that hour may not be detected the first time.

### Limits

- A conversation receives at most five automatic messages an hour and 20 in 24 hours, across all workflows and comment campaigns. This also stops two connected accounts from answering each other indefinitely.
- One person starts at most five runs of a workflow a day, whatever those runs went on to do, so they cannot use up its daily run limit or keep paid AI steps running. A later message appears as a stopped run that says why.
- Runs that failed, were stopped, or were skipped do not count toward the daily run limit, unless the workflow has an AI step, which costs money either way. When the limit is reached the workflow shows it, and later messages wait until the next day.
- Messages in a conversation someone took over never start a run.

Failed and unconfirmed sends remain inspectable. OpenPost does not repeat a send when the outcome is uncertain. One message gets at most one automatic reply, even if Meta delivers its webhook twice.

## Send follow-up messages

Add **Send follow-up messages** to send up to five ordered messages, each with its own delay. It works in two places:

- After **Instagram message received**, the sequence answers the message that started the run.
- After **Private comment reply**, the sequence waits for the person to respond in a direct message, then answers that response. A private reply is one message and does not open a conversation, so nothing further is sent until they respond. If no response arrives before the time you set, up to seven days, the run ends without sending and shows why.

The first delay counts from the person's response. Later delays count from the previous message. Every message must go out within 24 hours of that response, even if the person writes again. Delays that add up to 24 hours or more fail when you publish.

A conversation runs one automated sequence at a time, across all workflows. If someone sends another message while a sequence is running, or a second campaign reaches the same message, that later run sends nothing, finishes normally and shows why. Every message in a sequence counts toward the limits below, so a person who has just received other automated messages can get fewer of them, and the run stops and shows why.

OpenPost repeats the checks above before each message and also stops if someone takes over the comment. When a check fails, or a message fails or has an unconfirmed outcome, the sequence stops, nothing further is sent, and the run shows the reason. With review delivery, each message waits for its own approval when it is due. If the window closes while a message waits for approval, or 24 hours pass since the response, the approval ends, the run shows why, and the next message from that person can start a sequence.

## Send the link only to followers

A comment campaign can hold its link until the person follows your account. Start from **Workflows → Templates → Send a link to followers**, or open **Send follow-up messages** after a **Private comment reply** and turn on **Follow check**.

1. Write the private reply so it asks the person to follow and confirm, for example `If you follow us, tap "I followed" below`. Instagram shares whether someone follows you only after they message the account, so a comment alone is not enough.
2. In the private reply, set **Quick reply button** to the button the person taps, such as `I followed`, up to 20 characters. Tapping it sends that text as their message, which OpenPost reads whether it arrives by webhook or by the regular message check. With a quick reply button, only that exact text counts as the confirmation; other messages are ignored by the check, and opt-out and takeover still apply. Without one, any reply counts.
3. Write the first follow-up message. It appears above the link button, up to 640 characters.
4. Enter the **Button title**, up to 20 characters, and the **Button link**, which must start with `https://`. The page behind the link stays public; the check only decides who receives it.
5. Write the message for people who do not follow yet, for example `Follow us, then reply here again`.
6. Choose how many times to check, from 1 to 5. The default is 3.

When the person replies, OpenPost reads their follow status from Instagram:

- **Following.** The first follow-up goes out with the link button, and the rest of the sequence continues as usual.
- **Not following.** They receive your request to follow, with the same quick reply button. When they tap it, or reply when there is no button, within 24 hours of their last reply, OpenPost checks again. After the last check, the run ends without the link and shows why. On that last check, OpenPost sends nothing, because no further check would answer them.
- **Instagram cannot answer.** The run stops and shows the reason, for example missing permission, an account that blocked yours, or a network failure. OpenPost never treats an unanswered check as "not following", and sends neither the link nor the request to follow.

Instagram does not document quick replies on private replies. If Instagram rejects the button, the private reply fails, the run shows why, and OpenPost does not resend it without the button. Remove the **Quick reply button** and ask people to reply with a word instead.

Each reply is checked once. Replies reach OpenPost through webhooks or through the regular message check, so the follow check works before Meta webhooks are available. A link button opens the page in Instagram's browser and needs no webhook. The request to follow counts toward the limits above like any automated message, and a person taking over the conversation or the comment stops the check.

See [Meta's messaging documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-instagram-login/messaging-api) for provider requirements. This workflow does not include email capture or broadcasts.
